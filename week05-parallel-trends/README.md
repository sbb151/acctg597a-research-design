# Week 5: Parallel Trends as a Counterfactual Assumption

**Paper:** Schafhäutle and Veenman (2024), "Crowdsourced Forecasts and the Market Reaction to Earnings Announcement News," *The Accounting Review* 99(2): 421-456.

## The design problem

Schafhäutle and Veenman use a generalized difference-in-differences (DiD) design: outcomes are regressed on firm effects, year-quarter effects, and an indicator for firm-quarters after a firm is added to the Estimize platform (Table 5), and the same regression is estimated in event time with the year before coverage as the baseline (Figure 5). They state the identifying assumption accurately: "the trends in the outcome variables would have moved in tandem in the counterfactual situation that Estimize had not added a firm to the platform. Although we cannot formally test this assumption, an alternative solution is to explore whether the treatment (Estimize) and control (non-Estimize) firms exhibit similar pretrends" (p. 448). The module concerns the design, which is widely used, and not this paper in particular.

The general principle is an identity in potential outcomes. Let Y(1) and Y(0) denote a firm's outcome with and without treatment. Only treated firms after treatment reveal Y(1), so the DiD of observed means equals the average treatment effect on the treated (ATT) plus B, the difference between the two groups' trends in Y(0) from before to after treatment. Parallel trends is the statement B = 0, and B contains the mean Y(0) of treated firms after treatment, which no dataset records. The assumption is therefore not testable. In an event study with year -1 omitted, the coefficient for year k equals B_k for k < 0 and ATT_k + B_k for k >= 0, where B_k is the gap in Y(0) trends between year -1 and year k. A pre-trend test examines B_k before treatment; identification requires B_k = 0 after treatment; neither implies the other.

## The simulation modules

`parallel_trends.do` (base Stata, about five minutes because Module 2 is a Monte Carlo experiment) runs two self-contained modules with seed 2024. Because the data are simulated, Y(0) of treated firms after treatment is available, so the infeasible regression that does test parallel trends can be run beside the feasible one. Both modules use seven event years (k = -3 to 3), one treatment date (k = 0), firm and year effects, and firm-clustered standard errors. Identity checks are printed in the log and asserted.

1. **Three worlds: the pre-trend test and the assumption can disagree.** 4,000 firms, half treated. World 1: parallel trends holds and the ATT path is (0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.5). World 2: the ATT is zero and the Y(0) of treated firms rises by the same path from k = 0, so parallel trends fails; the observed panel is identical to World 1 row by row (maximum absolute difference 0), so every statistic coincides: the coefficient on Post is 0.39 (t = 16.2) and the joint F test of the two pre-period coefficients has p = 0.43 in both worlds, although the true effects are 0.40 and 0. World 3: parallel trends holds with the ATT of World 1, but treated firms experience a transitory shock of -0.25 at k = -3 that reverses at k = -2 (e.g., an accrual that reverses); the pre-period gaps average zero, so the coefficient on Post (0.39) and the event-study coefficients are unbiased, yet the pre-trend test rejects (F = 61.2, p < 0.001). The event-study figures plot the feasible coefficients (navy) against the coefficients with Y(0) as the outcome (gray and maroon). Output: `tables/tab_sv_parallel_trends_worlds.tex`, `figures/fig1a_sv_parallel_trends_w12.png`, `figures/fig1b_sv_parallel_trends_w3.png`.
2. **The pre-trend test as a screen.** 2,000 samples of 200 firms for each value of a drift gamma: the ATT is zero, and the Y(0) of treated firms drifts by gamma per year relative to control firms in every year, so that the expected event-study coefficient is gamma(k+1) and the expected coefficient on Post is 3.5 gamma. At gamma = 0.10 (in standard deviations of the error) the pre-trend F test rejects in 13 percent of samples, whereas the t-test on Post rejects the true ATT of zero in 90 percent; among the 87 percent of samples that pass the pre-test the rejection rate is also 90 percent. Both tests have size 0.05 at gamma = 0. The pre-test confronts the two smallest deviations (-2 gamma and -gamma), whereas the Post coefficient pools the full path (Roth 2022). ## Key readings

- Schafhäutle and Veenman (2024, *The Accounting Review*): the focal paper; finds that crowdsourced (Estimize) coverage is associated with lower mispricing of analyst forecast bias and lower earnings announcement premia, using the staggered addition of firms to the platform.
- Roth (2022, *American Economic Review: Insights*): shows that conventional pre-trend tests have low power against violations that would materially bias the estimates, and that conditioning on a passed pre-test can exacerbate the bias.
- Rambachan and Roth (2023, *Review of Economic Studies*): propose sensitivity analysis that bounds the post-treatment violation of parallel trends by a multiple of the pre-treatment violations; the bounds rest on an assumption linking the two periods.
- Kahn-Lang and Lang (2020, *Journal of Business & Economic Statistics*): argue that parallel pre-trends are neither necessary nor sufficient for the identifying assumption and that researchers should explain why trends would be parallel.
- Roth and Sant'Anna (2023, *Econometrica*): show that parallel trends is sensitive to functional form (e.g., it may hold in levels and fail in logs) unless a stronger distributional condition holds.
- Cunningham (2026, *Scott's Mixtape Substack*, "Parallel Trends is a Debiasing Method"): writes the DiD as the ATT plus a selection-bias term in first differences of Y(0), and distinguishes DiD as a calculation from DiD as a research design.
